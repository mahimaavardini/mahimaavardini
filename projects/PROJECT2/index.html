AI Security Research

Defensive Architectures Against Generative AI Exploitation

🧠Research Focus

  • Co-authored a research paper analyzing the security implications of generative AI systems, focusing on adversarial attacks such as data poisoning, model inversion, and automated phishing
  • Proposed an adaptive-learning defense model capable of autonomously retraining AI systems against evolving threats using real-time threat intelligence feeds
  • Designed a policy-driven cybersecurity architecture integrating NIST-compliant controls for AI data integrity and access governance
  • Investigated ethical and legislative countermeasures for AI-originated cyberattacks, recommending proactive monitoring, dataset validation, and exploit tracking systems to mitigate AI misuse
  • Synthesized cross-domain insights bridging AI governance, cybersecurity policy, and adversarial machine learning for enterprise-scale defense frameworks

⚠️Attack Vectors Analyzed

The research examined multiple adversarial attack methodologies targeting AI/ML systems:

  • Data Poisoning: Malicious dataset manipulation to corrupt model training and introduce backdoors
  • Model Inversion: Extracting sensitive training data through strategic query patterns
  • Automated Phishing: Leveraging generative AI to create convincing social engineering attacks
  • Model Theft: Replicating proprietary models through black-box query analysis
